A property's returns now come from its real lines (FLOW-453, decision [0245](decisions/0245-returns-from-the-lines.md), amending 0244). Only cash lines count, so a depreciation journal entry is out. The first rent payment splits investing from earning: the statement counts the lines from it on, and the money in the deal is what the property took before it (purchase and closing, renovation, and the holding costs, shown as their own line) less the net loan proceeds, so loan costs held back at closing count and a cash-out refinance lowers it. The purchase price comes from the purchase lines. When more came out than went in, cash-on-cash and the total return show ∞.
