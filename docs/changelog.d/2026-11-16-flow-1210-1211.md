FLOW-1210 and FLOW-1211: Company profit's date and amount orders get their lines sooner, from one read per direction, with one change a user can see besides speed: a switch to לפי פרויקט in the first moment, before the background project reads land, shows the loading skeleton (before this change the whole page waited for those reads). FLOW-1210: the all-lines read of each direction no longer waits for the two `get_breakdown(category)` reads (each row names its group, so it needs no key); it starts with the page, once the company currency is known, and the rows are filed into the categories when both are in (the per-category caches are seeded in either order, from an effect). The two `get_breakdown(project)` reads no longer hold `ready` in the category, date and amount orders, which never draw them (`projectsDrawn`); they start after the first paint (`useAfterFirstPaint`), so a switch to לפי פרויקט finds them in, and לפי פרויקט itself still waits for them. FLOW-1211: `get_breakdown_lines` with no key (and not kept-out, no project) and a null `p_limit` returns the whole direction in one computation and one call, where each page of 200 recomputed the direction (the migration `20261115190314_flow_1211_breakdown_lines_lean.sql` patches the function in place; `p_offset` is ignored for that read and `has_more` is false). The same read with a `p_limit` pages exactly as before, because the FLOW-1207 app stays on phones for hours or days after a deploy and the migration applies on merge, before it: it asks for pages of 200 by offset and parses every row with a schema that requires `category_name`, `currency` and `shared`. The new app sends `p_limit: null` (an omitted one defaults to 40). The no-key row, for either call, drops `category_id` (it is the key, null for none) and `kept_out` (always false), and keeps every other field, with `group_key`, the filing key. A keyed, kept-out or by-project read is unchanged and keeps every field (flow-mcp names them). The app parses the no-key row with its own schema (`breakdownLinesAllSchema`, in the new `packages/shared/src/breakdown-lines.ts`, split from `dashboard.ts` for its size limit) and, when it seeds a category's own cache, puts the `category_id` back, so the seeded rows equal the keyed read's. Should the database answer a page all the same (an older function that binds the null limit to its default of 40), the app reads the direction again in pages of 200 by offset, so the lines are always complete. pgTAP (`flow_1211_breakdown_lines_lean`, 25 checks, on a fixture with 242 generated lines on top of the base ones, so the expense direction is more than one page of 200 and the keyed read of repairs takes two pages) checks the 13 row keys, parity per category against the keyed read less the dropped fields and in order, the seeded shape, that a limit of 200 at offsets 0 and 200 gives disjoint pages that together equal the whole read, and that keyed, kept-out and by-project reads equal a copy of the body as it was; `flow_1207_breakdown_lines_all` follows the row. An app test parses a new-shape row with a copy of the FLOW-1207 schema (`breakdown-lines-compat.test.ts`; delete it once no FLOW-1207 app is in use). Measured locally (a fixture of 1,800 expense lines in one year on the local database, mean of 5, one session, not Flow QA; the machine's speed varied between runs, so both runs of the before are shown): the no-key read of the direction was 443.7 ms and 282.3 ms in 9 pages and 806,816 bytes (about 448 a row), and is 55.9 ms in one call (62.7 ms in the first run, before the row kept category_name, currency and shared); the bytes are 672,944 (about 374 a row), 17% fewer. Production before, as Flow QA, read-only, on 2026-11-16, against the old function (expense, USD): 2026 to date was 855 lines in 5 pages of 200, 268 ms total database time and 400,860 bytes; November was 17 lines in 1 page, 8.7 ms a call and 8,020 bytes.
