A new pgTAP test, `cross_company_sweep.test.sql` (FLOW-929), calls every function in `public` that the app and flow-mcp can call, read from the catalog, as another company's owner with the first company's ids as arguments: with its own company active, sending the first company's id as the company header, and with an old flow-mcp token it held for the first company. A last pass calls them as a viewer of the first company. It fails when a call changes the first company's rows or its users' and tokens' rows, returns or raises its ids, names or figures, copies them into the caller's company or the outbound HTTP queue, gives a different answer once the first company is deleted, or fails with anything but a company refusal; and when a function is left uncalled, so a new function is covered without an edit. Functions in `private` are out of scope, since PostgREST serves only `public`. It found no leak or cross-company write. CONTRIBUTING's Database privileges section explains the sweep and its four small allowlists (decision 0193).
