FLOW-1214: a forecast skip, reset or undo now moves the month's total at once. The write patches the `skips` the cached company inputs carry (the full answer and the slim one, seeded from it) with `setQueryData`, so the company next-month card and the overhead project's totals follow the tap, before the write answers. The app no longer refetches the company inputs after a skip, a reset or an undo (one `company_next_month_inputs` read fewer each time). A regular project's next-month page still updates after the write answers, because it reads `forecast_overrides_for`, not the inputs. A failed write puts the patch back, and the existing error toast and undo stay. Because an overlapping write's change is recorded at its tap, a failure can leave the cache ahead of the server: once every write of that month has settled and one of them failed, the inactive inputs are dropped and the active ones are read again, so the cache ends at the server's answer (a restore that fails partway is covered the same way). A reset clears that month's skips, and the undo of a reset puts them back. Inactive company inputs are still dropped, as FLOW-1201 does, and the month forecast is still refetched. The change is in `app/src/forecast-month.ts`, with a new `apply` option on `app/src/use-write.ts` (the write's undo runs on failure, before the optimistic snapshot is restored). Tests: `app/src/forecast-skip-patch.test.tsx` (new, 7 cases, including an overlapping-writes sequence with a refusal and a partial restore, both checked against the server's answer) and `app/src/forecast-month-keys.test.tsx`, which now expects no inputs read on set or reset; `app/src/use-write.test.tsx` covers `apply` (the undo runs before the snapshot restore, no undo on success, a failure with no `apply`). The new cases fail on main, and the three failure cases fail without the refetch. Measured on this branch: the bundle budget reports the Home entry at 243.8 of 248 KB gzip and all JavaScript at 734.8 of 740 KB. `load-report --speed 7` against main ran twice with different results: once (before the refetch change) the Home entry's JavaScript grew +0.4 KB gzip, all JavaScript +0.4 KB, and Home ready 1290 to 1407 ms (slower in 6 of 7 paired runs, +117 ms, 9%, under the gate's 15% relative rule); on the final commit the Home entry's JavaScript grew +0.6 KB gzip (allowance 2 KB), all JavaScript +0.6 KB, and Home ready 1163 to 1121 ms (slower in 1 of 7 paired runs). The two runs disagree, so the timing is noise on this container. No migration, no MCP tool change.
