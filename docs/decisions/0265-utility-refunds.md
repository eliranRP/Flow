# Utility bills the tenant never paid back are found on read, and the owner answers the rest

**Date:** 2026-11-09
**Status:** Accepted (the owner's plan of 2026-11-09, FLOW-1133; built in [FLOW-1134](../backlog/tasks/FLOW-1134.md). Builds on [0205](0205-transfer-match-suggestions.md) and [0185](0185-recurring-charge-per-project.md))

## Context

The company pays a property's utilities (electricity, water, gas, internet) and the tenant pays them back, either with a payment of their own or added to the rent. Nothing showed a bill the tenant never paid back. The owner's ask (2026-11-09): "see if we didn't get a refund on a utility payment from a tenant. It should wait a month from the charge and try to find a transaction that pay the amount we paid", with a way to mark a utility the company pays so it is never reviewed.

## Decision

1. **A utility** is one supplier's bills for one property and currency, the key of a recurring charge ([0185](0185-recurring-charge-per-project.md)). Flow finds it from the lines: an expense filed to a property in a category named חשבונות or under it (the parent tree, as the starter categories set it up), or an expense paid with a card whose name names exactly one property and carries a utilities word (`utilities` or `utility`), wherever that line is filed: that supplier is then a utility of the property the card names. The card rule is decision [0262](0262-jev-card-name-project.md)'s, ported to SQL (`private.card_name_project_of`, with the same generic words, tokens and cases as `cardProject` in `jev_tag_plan.ts`; a comment in each file and a test in each with the same cases keep them in step): every word of the project's name is in the card's name, ignoring case, numbers under two digits, words under 3 letters and the generic words; a number on both must agree; overhead and finished projects are not candidates; two matches are none. The owner can add a supplier by hand (`utility_payers.added`). A bill is a posted expense line of the utility that is not a credit note and is more than one unit of its currency (over 100 minor units), so a payment of a few cents never matches it.
2. **Who pays.** `public.utility_payers` keeps the owner's pick per utility: the tenant pays it back (`tenant`, the default when there is no row) or the company pays (`company`). A company-paid utility's bills are never checked, which also closes its open ones. `list_utility_payers` gives each utility with its usual amount, the median of its last 3 bills, and its latest bill's category, worked out on read.
3. **The match is worked out on read** (`private.utility_refund_rows`), as the transfer suggestions are ([0205](0205-transfer-match-suggestions.md)); the sync does nothing new. Every read takes an as-of date (default Israel's today; the tests always pass one). For each bill of a tenant-paid utility, from the first one in the books, oldest first, Flow looks for a money-in line (income):
   - dated from one month before the bill to the as-of date;
   - filed to the same property, or to no property;
   - in the bill's currency, not already the refund of another bill, and not a pair the owner rejected;
   - whose amount is within one unit of the currency (100 minor units, $1 or ₪1) of (1) the bill, (2) several of the property's open bills of that month together (any 2 or 3 of the month's first 12 open bills, or the whole month's when it has more than 3), or (3) the property's usual rent plus one or more of those bills (the same groups).
   The property's **usual rent** is the median of its last 3 money-in lines filed under שכירות (or a category under it) in that currency, dated before the bill.
   Among the candidates, a match that counts by itself comes first, then the lower rule, the closer date, the smaller group and the smaller difference. One payment is the refund of one bill, or of one group of bills (rules 2 and 3).
   A supplier's credit note (an expense line marked credit) is money from the supplier, not the tenant, so it is never a candidate.
   **One pass** (the review of #1094): the bills the owner already closed are left out first; every group's targets (its total, and the usual rent plus its total) meet the money-in lines once, joined by amount buckets 100 minor units wide; then the bills are matched oldest first in one loop. On a synthetic company with 2,000 utility bills and about 39,000 lines (10 properties, 40 months, the noise lines dense in every amount), `count_utility_refunds` takes about 200 to 260 ms and the app's `list_utility_refunds` about 250 ms on the local database. An index on a company's lines by direction and date (`transactions_company_direction_date_idx`) serves the money-in candidates.
4. **What Flow does with a match.** Rule 1 or 2 with the payment filed to the bill's category counts as **refunded by itself** (`source: auto`). For rule 2 every bill of the group must share that category; when the bills' categories differ, the match is a suggestion (the coordinator's pick, 2026-11-09: fewer false autos). Any other match, and every rule 3 match, is a **suggestion** the owner answers. A bill with no match 30 days or more after its date is **not refunded** (לא הוחזר); a younger one is waiting. A bill whose property had no rent line in the bill's month is flagged (חודש בלי שכירות). Auto matches are not stored: they are worked out again on every read.
5. **The answers.** `public.utility_refund_answers` keeps, per bill, `matched` with the payment line (the owner's yes), `rejected` with the payment line (never matched again for that bill), or `seen` (closed, nothing to do). RLS is on with no client grant; only the functions read and write it. `answer_utility_refund(bill, state, payment, as_of, keep_category)` (owner or editor, [0167](0167-team-members.md)) writes one answer; `null` takes the bill's answer back. With a matched payment filed elsewhere, `keep_category` true is the card's להשאיר: the books stay as they are and the list no longer raises the wrong-category note (false takes the keep back). Taking back a refund Flow counted by itself records the pair as `rejected`, so the next read neither counts nor suggests it again. `mark_utility_refunds_seen(property)` closes every open not-refunded bill of a property at once (suggestions stay open) and returns them, and `unmark_utility_refunds_seen` is its ביטול.
6. **The one-tap fix.** When the owner matched a payment filed elsewhere (rent, other income), the books stay as they are and the bill shows `wrong_category`. `refile_utility_refund(bill, payment)` moves the payment to the bill's category and property through `reassign_transaction` when the payment is the bills it refunds and they are of one category. Otherwise it splits the payment in one call through `save_line_split` (the line-parts writer of [0232](0232-ledger-only-books.md)): one part per bill category on the bill's property, and, when the payment is more than the bills by over one unit of the currency (more than 100 minor units: rent plus the bills), the rest staying in its own category. What it changed is kept on every answer that matched the payment (`refile`), so the undo works from either bill. `undo_utility_refile` puts it back, and is refused (`line changed since`) once the line was changed after it. A refiled refund keeps its answer until the refile is undone.
7. **Reads and MCP ([0095](0095-mcp-first.md)).** `list_utility_refunds(property, as_of, all)` and `count_utility_refunds(as_of)` are read by any member. Every call that takes an id checks it against the caller's company. MCP has `list_utility_refunds`, `count_utility_refunds` and `list_utility_payers`, plus four writes, each with the idempotency key, the write rate limit and undo by the write's id. The writes are `set_utility_payer` (undo kind `utility_payer`), `answer_utility_refund` (`utility_refund`: the bill's answers before), `mark_utility_refunds_seen` (`utility_refunds_seen`: every bill it closed) and `refile_utility_refund` (`utility_refile`). Each undo is a conflict once what it wrote changed again.

8. **The category roots match by part of the name** (FLOW-1141, 2026-11-09): the rent is every income category whose name starts with the word שכירות, and the bills' tree every expense category whose name starts with the word חשבונות, after an optional number and an optional הכנסות or הוצאות ("הכנסות שכירות" and "12 חשבונות שוטפים" match; "ביטול שכירות", "הכנסה משכירות מחסן" and "שירותי הנהלת חשבונות" do not), each with every category under it.
9. **Exact matches only, and no suggestions** (FLOW-1143, the owner, 2026-11-09). This replaces the matching rules of items 3 and 4. A bill counts as refunded by itself only when a money-in line of exactly its amount (rule 1), or of exactly the sum of several of the same month's bills of one category (rule 2), is filed to the bills' property and to their category, dated from one month before the bill to the as-of date. Flow suggests nothing: rule 3 (the usual rent plus bills), amount matches in another category and payments with no property are gone, there is no `suggested` state, and `count_utility_refunds` keeps `suggested_count` at 0 for the app. Every other bill is not refunded 30 days after its date. The owner's own answers stay: picking a payment as the refund (in any category, with the wrong-category note, the fix and להשאיר), seen, company-paid, refile and undo.
10. **A split payment counts by its parts** (FLOW-1146, the owner, 2026-11-10). A money-in line split by category meets the bills by each part: its amount, its category and its project (the line's when the part has none), with the same exact rules 1 and 2. A split line's whole amount matches nothing. An unsplit line counts by its whole, as before. A rent line split into the rent and the water bill the tenant paid with it now refunds that water bill. `list_utility_months` gives each rent line its parts, and a split line with a rent part counts as rent.

## Alternatives rejected

**Match in the sync and store the matches.** A match depends on lines from any later sync, on the owner's answers and on edits to either line; a stored match would need a job to keep it right. Read time is cheap: a company's utility bills and its money-in lines.

**Count every amount match by itself.** The owner's rule: an amount alone may be a coincidence (rent of the same size, another tenant). Only the amount and the bill's category together count without asking.

**Refile the payment when the owner says yes.** The owner chose to keep the books as they are and show a note with one button, so a yes never moves money between categories on its own.

**Store the usual amount and the usual rent.** Both change with every bill and rent line; the median of the last few, worked out on read, is never stale.

## Consequences

- Nothing changes in the books until the owner taps the fix. A company-paid utility, a seen bill and a rejected pair stay as answered across syncs.
- The whole history is checked from the first bill, so the first read may list many bills not refunded; "mark all as seen" per property clears them at once.
- Auto matches are recomputed on read, so a later line can change which payment Flow counts for a bill that the owner never answered.

## Known limits and follow-ups

- **The card-name rule exists twice**, in Deno for Jev and in SQL for the utilities. Changing one means changing the other; the shared cases in both tests catch a drift in the cases they cover.
- **Groups are capped**: pairs and triples from a month's first 12 open bills (220 triples at most), plus the whole month. A refund of 4 bills out of a month of 5 is not found; the whole month of 5 is.
- **A month's bills in two currencies** are grouped per currency.
