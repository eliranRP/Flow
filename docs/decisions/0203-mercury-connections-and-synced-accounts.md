# Several Mercury connections, the accounts each one syncs, and the date each account imports from

**Date:** 2026-10-21
**Status:** Accepted
**Amends:** [0086](0086-mercury.md) (one Mercury connection per company; the own-account set is every org account)

## Context

[0086](0086-mercury.md) gave a company one Mercury connection, keyed `(company_id, provider)` in `connector_connections`, and imported every account the key could see: the own-account set was every id from `GET /accounts`, `GET /credit` and `GET /treasury`, rebuilt before every sync. Two things came up. An owner with two companies' Mercury (one key each) could connect only one. And every account came in, a Treasury investment account and a personal or payroll account included, with no way to leave one out; a new account Mercury opened was imported without asking. The approved board round (canvas note "MERCURY: CHOOSE WHICH ACCOUNTS SYNC, AND FROM WHICH DATE", boards SettingsMercuryPick, SettingsMercuryFrom, SettingsMercuryAccounts, SettingsMercuryNewAccount, SettingsMercuryMulti, FLOW-938) asks for: a step after the key that picks the accounts and the import date, an editable synced accounts list, a question when Mercury reports a new account, and several connections per company.

## Decision

- **Slots.** `connector_connections` gets `slot` (1 to 9; SUMIT stays on 1) and its key becomes `(company_id, provider, slot)`. A Mercury connection also keeps `org_label` (the organisation's name, Mercury's `legalBusinessName` on its accounts), `accounts_chosen_at`, `sync_account_ids` (null: every listed account), `skip_account_ids` (accounts the owner left out) and `account_from` (`{account id: day}` for an account added after the connection's start). The migration marks every existing Mercury row chosen and synced on every account it lists, so it keeps syncing exactly what it synced.
- **A key goes to a slot.** `mercury-connect` puts a key that lists an account of an existing connection on that connection (the same organisation, a new token); `slot` names the connection a replaced key is for; any other key is a new connection on the next free slot. A key that lists another connection's account is refused (`already_connected`); a tenth connection is refused (`too_many_connections`). A new connection waits: `accounts_chosen_at` is null and nothing syncs until the owner chooses. A new key that lists other accounts than before clears the cursor; when it still lists some of the connection's accounts (a rotated key of the same organisation) the owner's choices stay, an account it no longer lists drops out of the synced and skipped lists, and an account it lists for the first time is asked about as a new account. Only a key that lists none of the synced accounts makes the owner choose again. The key form no longer asks for a date for Mercury (SUMIT keeps its own).
- **Choosing the accounts.** `set_mercury_accounts(p_slot, p_accounts, p_from)` (owner only, `security definer`, granted to `authenticated`) saves the synced accounts of one connection: at least one, each listed by that connection. Every listed account left out goes to `skip_account_ids`, so it is not offered again as new. `p_from` is `start`, a day not after today, or null (the first choice starts at the start; an account added later starts today). Accounts already synced keep their start. `import_from` becomes the earliest start of any synced account, and a later start is kept per account in `account_from`. An account added with a start before today clears the connection's cursor, so the next sync reads back to it; the rows already stored are updated, never doubled (the upsert is keyed by the Mercury transaction id).
- **What syncs.** `mercury-sync` claims all of a company's Mercury connections and syncs each chosen one in turn, each with its own key, cursor (`upsert_connector_lines(..., p_slot)`), failure (`note_connector_failure(..., p_slot)`) and stamp (`stamp_connector_sync(..., p_slot)`). One connection's rejected key or hold does not stop the others; the counts add up. The engine's own-account set is no longer every org account: it is the synced accounts of every connection of the company, so a transfer between two synced accounts is skipped even across connections when Mercury names the other account. A line on an account no one syncs is skipped (`unsynced_account`), a card account's too, instead of failing the run. A transfer from a synced account to one no one syncs is imported as money out, hinted to `העברות` like a transfer to an own account that is not connected (0086). When that account syncs later (the answer yes, the synced accounts list, `set_bank_accounts`), the next run skips the same line as a transfer between synced accounts (`own_account_transfer`, `internal_transfer`, `treasury_transfer`) and voids its stored copy, so no one-sided money-out line stays. Lines dated before their account's own start are left out. Stored lines are rechecked and voided only by the connection whose accounts they are on (lines with no account, from before FLOW-304, by the first chosen connection), so one key never voids a line only another key can see.
- **A new account.** An account Mercury lists that is neither synced nor skipped is new: the connected page asks "לסנכרן אותו ל־Flow?". Yes adds it with the connection's own start (a new account has little history); no adds it to the skipped accounts. Nothing of it syncs before the answer.
- **One connection at a time.** `disconnect_connector(p_provider, p_slot)` deletes one connection (no slot: every connection of the provider, as before). `connector_skips` has the slot too: a complete run replaces only its own connection's skip records. The daily refresh queues one request per company and provider however many connections it has. The status view `connector_connection_status` lists one row per connection with `slot`, `org_label`, `accounts_chosen`, `sync_account_ids` and `skip_account_ids`.
- **MCP ([0095](0095-mcp-first.md)).** `list_bank_connections` reads every connection with its accounts (name, last 4, kind, balance), which sync, which were left out, which are new, and where each synced one starts. `set_bank_accounts` (the step and the synced accounts list) and `answer_bank_account` (yes or no to a new account) save through `set_mercury_accounts`, so the rules stay in one place: the owner only, with the idempotency key, the write rate limit and undo kind `bank_accounts` on the connection's id (it puts the accounts, the skipped list and the starts back, and clears the cursor when what comes back reads further back, by the same rule as `set_mercury_accounts`; lines already imported stay). Connecting, replacing and disconnecting a key stay in the app: the assistant never handles a key.
- **Deploy order.** CI deploys `mercury-connect` and `mercury-sync` with the SUMIT functions before `cd-push.sh` pushes the migration (that order is for the SUMIT reseal and stays). Until the migration is in, and for as long as a failed push leaves it out, both functions run on the old schema: they read `connector_connections` with every column, treat a row without `slot` or `accounts_chosen_at` as the one connection on slot 1 that syncs every account its key lists, call the RPCs without `p_slot` (and retry without it when PostgREST has no such function), and leave out the new columns from their writes; `mercury-connect` saves the import date the old app sends, as before (`connectors/mercury/legacy.ts`).
- **Deliberately not added:** a per-account transaction count estimate, and an entry point to move an already synced account's start earlier.

## Alternatives rejected

**A second table for extra connections.** It would duplicate the sealed key, the cursor, the claim and every sync RPC's connection read for the second and later connections, and two code paths for the same sync.

**Sync every account, hide the unchecked ones in the app.** Their lines would still count in totals that read the ledger directly (P&L, forecasts, MCP), and a transfer to an unchecked account would vanish as an own-account skip.

**Clear the cursor on any change.** Unchecking an account or adding one from today needs no re-read; clearing would re-read up to two years for nothing.

**Ask Mercury's `GET /organization` for the name.** Not in the generated API or the allowlist; every account already carries `legalBusinessName`.

## Consequences

The connected page loses its accounts and card facts and gains the synced accounts row ("3 מתוך 5"). Settings › חיבורים says "2 חיבורים" on the Mercury row with more than one connection. With two or more, `/settings/connections/mercury` opens on their list; `?slot=N` opens one, `?add=1` adds one, `?edit=1` edits the synced accounts. Onboarding's Mercury sheet asks only for the key and then shows the same accounts step in a sheet. Rows from before keep syncing unchanged; their next sync stores the account kinds, balances and the organisation's name. `set_import_from` still writes every Mercury row of the company; the app no longer calls it for Mercury.

MCP: `list_bank_connections`, `set_bank_accounts` and `answer_bank_account` do what the screens do ([TOOLS.md](../mcp/TOOLS.md)). `sync_bank` syncs every chosen connection and answers `not_found` / `bank is not connected` when none is chosen; after `set_bank_accounts` adds an account, `sync_bank` brings its lines in now, else the daily sync does.
